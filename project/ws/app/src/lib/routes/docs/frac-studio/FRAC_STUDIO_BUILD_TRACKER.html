Where we are Built end to end on Claude; now complete testing, then one deploy

The portal, the proxy route and the service with its AI layer are built, committed and pushed. The whole Creator and Reviewer flow runs on Claude Sonnet 5.5: a standard FRAC costs about $0.95 and takes about 4 minutes (three complete FRACs run, $3.88 billed 6–9 Nov). Since 6 Nov: review rules tuned (sentences that rule an act out are no longer Blockers, "family planning" is not a KCM name), the expert check asks for a re-run after a fix or an edit, one running job per FRAC, a $3 spending limit per job, and cost and review reports. On 11 Nov the eight review decisions were built: documents and templates, position matching, the agent choosing the size, a roles preview popup, View Source links, a draft Excel before the expert check, and "Agent" and "Position" wording. They are not committed yet. With standard sheets a FRAC can cost about $0.30 instead of $0.96. What remains is clinical sign-off, the real-workbook acceptance tests and the in-app checks below.

What each product step can do today

The steps a user walks through in the spec. Green works end to end now. Amber works by hand or without the AI review, and gets AI drafting later. Red needs the AI layer.

Create FRAC

spec §5 · 5 of 6 usable
  1. 1
    Brief

    Position, state and scope, plus an optional job description or order. The position is matched exactly against your FRACs, then Positions, and a match can supply the postings and roles. The agent chooses the size. Locks once research exists.

    Works
  2. 2
    Job research

    "Research the job" or fill in by hand; revise with feedback. Reads attached documents first (1 search, not 3). A Sources sheet turns search off. Sources link to the pages search opened (View source). Imported FRACs skip it.

    Works (Claude)
  3. 3
    Roles & activities

    AI drafts in batches, then edit by hand; team-added activities survive a revise. With a Roles sheet or a matched position, only empty roles get activities.

    Works (Claude)
  4. 4
    Competencies

    Derive / re-derive with AI, every activity mapped; editable table with Used by and D/F/B counts. With a Competencies sheet the AI only maps activities to it.

    Works (Claude)
  5. 5
    Level ladders

    Write missing, rewrite all, or revise one competency; edit any level by hand. Ladders from a sheet are kept. "Preview roles" opens a popup of each role with its competencies and levels once every activity is mapped.

    Works (Claude)
  6. 6
    Expert check & download

    Rule-based checks plus the agent's quoted checklist; "Fix these competencies" (ladders only for now); "Download draft Excel" beside the check to confirm the file first; after a fix or edit the check asks for a re-run, and a download meanwhile is a draft without the old findings.

    Works (Claude)

How a review runs, first to last

The Reviewer upload and the Creator's expert check enter separately, become the same review model, and run through one engine. Everything above the AI box is counted in code and gives the same answer every time.

Every computed check

Severity: B Blocker, M Major, m Minor. Checks marked "FRAC sheet" run only when the workbook has one.

CheckRuleSeverity
Bloom's verbs A level label starts with a non-measurable verb (Understands, Knows, Learns, Is aware of, Appreciates, Is familiar with, Realises, Comprehends). Counted per level; adds "Levels 1 and 2 barely differ" when both are hit. M if the worst level hits ≥25% of competencies, else m
Labels are clean but an indicator starts with one of those verbs. m
FRAC structure Activities start with a syllabus verb (Describes, Explains, Lists, Defines, States, Discusses…). FRAC sheet. M at ≥30% of activities, else m
A role has fewer than 2 activities. FRAC sheet. m
No competency is typed Domain, Functional or Behavioural. M
No Behavioural or no Functional competencies (KCM has 13 B + 21 F). M
Level ladder A competency has fewer than 5 level labels. M above 10% of competencies, else m
The same L5 label appears on 3 or more competencies (one finding per label). M
One competency repeats a label across two of its levels. m
Coverage & sources Activities with no competency mapping; the finding quotes the first 3. FRAC sheet. M at ≥5% of activities, else m
Competencies no activity or role maps to. FRAC sheet. m
Mappings point to codes missing from the competency sheet. FRAC sheet. M
Some, but not all, activities have every posting column blank. FRAC sheet. m
Guidelines & context Repealed laws: Lunacy Act or "lunatic", Mental Health Act 1987, NDPS … 1965. Searched across activities, competencies and every level. One finding per pattern, up to 12 codes listed. B
TT or tetanus toxoid (now Td, 2018). M
Old programme names: RNTCP, NPCDCS, HWC / Health & Wellness Centre, NVBDCP, MCTS, MTP Act 1971 unless "2021" appears nearby. m
Scope (r3) ANM and ASHA acts beyond the cadre's authorised scope, sentence by sentence (config/review-rules.json). Sentences that rule the act out ("does not … inject", "without … injections") or refer it are not flagged. B
Breadth and KCM (r3) Catch-all, scheme-list or above-the-post labels (too broad); Domain labels that are KCM names ("family / birth planning" excluded); ladders shifted upward. M / m
Coverage (r3) Core cadre areas with no dedicated competency; the r3 outdated names (NRT, RMNCH+A, NRHM, VHND, Five Rights, partograph without LCG, device law for frontline). M / m
AI checklist Claude Vidya answers fixed questions per competency: Q1 an act beyond scope, Q2 a wrong drug, dose, law or programme fact, Q3 state fit. Every flag must quote at least 4 words of that competency; at most 3 AI findings, fixed titles. B M m
